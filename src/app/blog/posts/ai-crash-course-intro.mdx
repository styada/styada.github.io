# Introduction

With the popularity of ChatGPT and LLMs in general reaching new peaks, our world as we know it is changing forever. Prior to the wildfire-esque spread of 
generative AI to households across the world, the first thoughts that came to the minds of non-techy folks when artificial intelligence was mentioned
were either the self-driving cars that were the epitome of AI back then or the time they randomly clicked on a Boston Dynamics humanoid robot demo
video on YouTube - often times followed by looking up the 2004 "I, Robot" movie starring Will Smith and wondering if robots will rise up and take over.
The overall sentiment from the tech industry and community at large was that AI, at the time, was just a bunch of "if-else" expressions strung together.
For all these maybes, one thing was certain for both sides - that it wasn't a matter of "if" but a question of "when" AI would become common place in the
lives and homes of everyone. So far this certainity has proven to be true to some degree, if a person has access to a device that can connect to Wi-Fi,
they already can have access to some of the world's most complex models with hundreds of billions of parameters. 

Still, while AI chatbots have become popular and well-known for their usefulness, the vast majority of people don't understand them well enough and remain
skeptical. To top it off, there have been dozens of important and industry shifting developments being made every couple of months making it difficult for folks
to keep up even if they wanted to. As someone who has the privilege of working on all things GenAI on a full-time basis for a couple of years now, I have noticed
the lack of general knowledge in most people about just how deep this rabbit hole we call GenAI goes. As a result of this, I am putting together a string of blog posts hand-crafted just for the average joe to start to understand this technology without the barriers
of entry. My goal is more to have a clear discussion with you rather than to write a textbook and throw a bunch of formulas at you.
